## 1. Webull terms: automation, sharing, servers, messaging, data, revocation

**What the public documents say**
- Webull Terms of Service (https://www.webull.com/protocol/webull_terms_of_service): §2 "Users agree not to provide, transfer, or sell their account to any third-party for use"; account "can only be used by registered users"; Webull "reserves its right to reclaim the account and temporarily/permanently suspend the account at any time and in its absolute discretion"; §3.1(d) users must not use the Products "in any way that inhibits Webull's ability to provide the Products to other users"; §3.3 Webull "may suspend any of the Products at any time and in its sole discretion"; §3.5 market data must be used "in accordance with terms and conditions of the third-party data providers". No explicit bot/VPS prohibition appears in the ToS; the OpenAPI is a sanctioned channel.
- Webull help "Application Programming Interfaces (APIs)" (https://www.webull.com/help/faq/10512-Application-Programming-Interfaces-APIs): API application requires an active brokerage account, **minimum net account value $100**, and is "reviewed based on your risk profile and trading history"; "Keep your App Key and App Secret secure and do not expose them to any third party"; keys can be reset any time; applicants must "confirm that you have read and accepted the agreement" during registration.
- Developer FAQ (https://developer.webull.com/apis/docs/faq/): IP whitelisting is "a security feature available to institutional clients"; no extra charge for API; OpenAPI market-data subscriptions are purchased separately; first-time 2FA loop 300 s.
- Token docs (https://developer.webull.com/apis/docs/authentication/token/): token becomes **INVALID if "no API calls made for 15 consecutive days"**; no refresh endpoint; 2FA (app SMS approval) is required only at creation; an invalid token means "create a new Token and start the process again" (i.e., a new in-app approval).
- Rate limits page (already in plan): repeated 429 abuse leads to temporary IP block.

**Corrections / additions to the plan**
1. The actual **OpenAPI agreement text is only shown inside the Webull portal at application time** and is not published; its automated-trading, redistribution and revocation clauses are **UNVERIFIED**. Action: save a copy of the agreement at application; add to the repo's compliance folder.
2. Plan §1.1 says the 2FA token is "persisted". True, but it **dies after 15 idle days** and needs a human with the Webull app to re-approve. Add a daily authenticated keepalive call and a Telegram alert on token INVALID, and keep a phone with the Webull app reachable.
3. Account sharing: a personal account may not be operated by anyone else (ToS §2); Kalshi Rule 3.5(f)(a) additionally requires the FCM to restrict order-entry system access to "that individual FCM Customer" (rulebook below). Do not let a co-founder operate the bot on your account.
4. Market data: OpenAPI quote subscriptions carry third-party-provider terms; redistribution (e.g., publishing a public dashboard of Webull quotes) is outside those terms. Kalshi public REST is a safer source for anything you publish.
5. Webull can suspend products (event contracts) "at any time and in its sole discretion" (ToS §3.3). Given the state actions in §4 below, treat catalog withdrawal by state as a live operational risk.

## 2. Kalshi rulebook provisions that bind an FCM (Webull) customer

Source: KalshiEX LLC Rulebook v1.29 (https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/regulatory/rulebook/Kalshi%20DCM%20Rulebook%20v.1.29.pdf), text extracted locally; Robinhood mirror of the same rulebook at https://cdn.robinhood.com/assets/robinhood/legal/KalshiEX_LLC_Rulebook.pdf.

- **Rule 3.3(a)**: FCMs must document that FCM Customers "are aware of and acknowledge this Rule and this Rulebook and Source Agency Prohibition."
- **Rule 3.3(b)**: "FCM Customers are allowed to have accounts with multiple FCMs. However, an FCM Customer must not intentionally match its own orders against each other ... Before an FCM Customer trades on Kalshi with multiple accounts, it must disclose the identity of each account to the Exchange by email." Webull's FAQ 11052 gives the address: "If you hold an existing Kalshi account, notify both teams at rule33@kalshi.com" (https://www.webull.com/help/faq/11052-All-about-Event-Contracts). **Plan §6 Phase 5 (direct-Kalshi executor) and §8 Q5 must include this disclosure step and a self-match guard across both executors.**
- **Rule 3.3(e)**: "An FCM Customer must adhere to all relevant Position Limits. This includes if the FCM trades through multiple FCMs or IBs, or is also a Self-Clearing Member." **Rule 5.19(f)**: position limits apply to all accounts a person "directly or indirectly holds positions or controls trading" and to positions held by persons "acting pursuant to an express or implied agreement". Limits aggregate across Webull + Kalshi direct.
- **Rule 3.3(f)**: if the customer's FCM account goes negative, "the FCM may liquidate some or all of the FCM Customer's positions."
- **Rule 3.5(e)**: FCMs must ensure customers adhere to Position Limits "and establishing automated pre-trade risk controls" (so Webull-side rejections are expected, even if not documented). **Rule 3.5(f)(a)**: FCM must restrict the order-entry system for an individual customer to "that individual FCM Customer".
- **Rule 5.11 (error trades)**: review requests must reach Kalshi within **15 minutes** of execution and before expiration; Kalshi computes fair value and a **±20¢ "No Cancellation Range"**; trades outside it may be cancelled/adjusted; the trader "whose order resulted in the cancellation or adjustment shall pay $3,000"; decisions "final and not subject to appeal". **5.11(d)**: the automated-trading-system-malfunction exception is only available to Self-Clearing Members with a market-maker agreement or "an advanced tier of Kalshi's API" — **not to FCM customers**. A Webull bot's fat-finger has no safety valve.
- **Rule 5.12**: if Kalshi suspends/revokes an FCM (Webull), "any open Order ... shall be canceled" and subsequent trades are invalid.
- **Rule 5.13** FCM recordkeeping; **Rule 5.17(y)/(z)**: Insider/MNPI and "influence on the outcome" prohibitions (Source Agency employees barred); **(aa)** "spoofing", violating bids/offers, reckless disregard during the closing period; **(bb)** "money pass", "wash trade", "front-running"; **(cc)** anti-fraud. Self-clearing members must have "adequate controls ... to prevent excessive messaging or other activity that may be deemed detrimental or disruptive" (Rule 5.x order-entry section); the FCM carries that duty for its customers.
- **Rule 5.18/5.19**: Position Accountability Levels on all contracts; above them Kalshi can demand information, freeze increases, and **liquidate**; first limit breach → warning letter, second within 12 months → disciplinary proceeding. FCMs report customers over "Reportable Levels" to Kalshi at a regular cadence.
- **Chapter 4 Market Makers**: designation is for Members only, by agreement; benefits include "reduced fees, differing Position Limits" and 10× accountability levels. Not reachable through Webull.
- **Rule 7.1 Market Outcome Review**: Outcome Review Committee decides "within a 24 hour period"; settlement occurs on the determination date; **Rule 7.2**: Kalshi may swap the Source Agency/Underlying after listing (relevant to the plan's Google-Finance vs Kalshi index-source note).
- **BTC contract terms** (https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/contract_terms/BTC.pdf): Underlying = simple average of BRTI over the 60 s before `<time>`; "If no data is available on the Expiration Date at the Expiration Time, then the market resolves to No"; **Position Limit $1,000,000 per strike, per Member** (plan's $7M figure is the S&P series only); Settlement no later than the day after expiration unless under Rule 7.1 review.
- Insider-trading enforcement is active: CFTC Enforcement advisory 2026-02-25 (https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9185-26) and Kalshi/CFTC actions April 22–23, 2026 (https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/cftc-and-kalshi-announce-enforcement-actions-targeting-prediction-markets-fctm). For index/crypto hourlies the practical exposure is Rule 5.17(y) "Source Agency" affiliation (CF Benchmarks, S&P DJI) — a compliance attestation the operator should record.
- Incidents: Kalshi halted all trading 2026-04-15 until 15:55 ET (https://gvwire.com/2026/04/15/kalshi-trading-temporarily-halted-website-shows/); a Michigan court ordered Kalshi to void/refund Michigan residents' trades and the CFTC ordered Kalshi not to comply (https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/07/14/u-s-cftc-moves-to-stop-kalshi-from-canceling-trades-as-ordered-by-michigan-court). Add "exchange halt" and "court-ordered trade cancellation" to the risk register; the bot must tolerate cancelled fills in reconciliation.

## 3. Tax treatment and record-keeping

- **No IRS guidance exists** on event contracts (Camuso CPA analysis: https://camusocpa.com/section-1256-prediction-market-tax/ — "no revenue rulings or formal pronouncements"; NATP: https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/prediction-market-contracts-are-showing-up-on-client-returns/ via search summary).
- **Section 1256 (60/40, mark-to-market, Form 6781, no wash sales)** is described by multiple practitioners as an aggressive position: the swap exclusion §1256(b)(2)(B) is in tension with Kalshi's own litigation stance that its contracts are swaps (https://www.convoyfinance.com/resources/kalshi-polymarket-taxes-llc-section-1256, https://coselite.com/blog/section-1256-event-contracts); Keeper recommends **Form 8275 disclosure** if you take it (https://www.keepertax.com/posts/how-to-file-taxes-on-kalshi-and-polymarket). If §1256 applies, wash-sale rules do not (Form 6781/Pub 550, https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-6781).
- **Forms actually issued**: Kalshi issues 1099-INT/1099-MISC (rewards)/1099-B (crypto transfers)/1099-DA but **no 1099-B for event-contract trades**; Robinhood states "event contract trades aren't reported to the IRS by Robinhood" (Monaco CPA, https://www.monacocpa.cpa/post/prediction-market-taxes-kalshi-polymarket-robinhood). Webull FAQ 11052 says only that it reports "using required forms (1099 or other relevant documents)". **Which form Webull Futures LLC issues for event contracts (1099-B vs 1099-MISC vs none) is UNVERIFIED** — ask futures@webull.com in writing before Phase 2.
- **Wagering characterization risk**: OBBBA §70114 amended §165(d) so that for tax years beginning after 2025 only **90% of wagering losses** are deductible, and only against wins (https://www.foster.com/larry-s-tax-law/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-part-3-gambling-code-section-165-d). For a high-turnover bot this is severe: $50k wins / $50k losses → $5k phantom income. Whether CFTC event contracts are "wagering transactions" is unsettled; a repeal proposal is pending (https://www.casino.org/news/odds-improve-tax-deduction-gambling-losses-will-be-restored/). The plan's §7 "Tax: 1099 reporting" one-liner understates this.
- **Wash sales under capital treatment**: §1091 applies to "stock or securities"; practitioners have not resolved whether event contracts are covered (**UNVERIFIED**). A hold-to-settlement bot that re-buys the same strike next hour would be exposed if §1091 applied; design the journal to compute both views.
- **Record-keeping for thousands of trades**: keep a transaction-level export with contract ticker, side/outcome, open/close timestamps, price, quantity, exchange fee and Webull fee separately, settlement value, and the Webull event-account statement number; Webull's event account has its own account number and statements (FAQ 11052). Form 8949 permits summary reporting with an attached statement; Form 6781 aggregates. Reconcile monthly against the E-Document Center statements (https://www.webull.com/help/faq/10993-Common-Tax-Form-Questions).
- State tax: nothing event-contract-specific found (**UNVERIFIED**).

## 6. Security and operations

**Where the endpoints live (measured today from this box)**
- `api.webull.com` and `api.elections.kalshi.com` resolve to **CloudFront** edges (AWS ip-ranges: service CLOUDFRONT); pinging them measures an edge, not the matching engine.
- `events-api.webull.com` (gRPC order/position events) → 13.216.26.136 / 3.216.39.20 / 35.170.158.247 = **AWS us-east-1 (N. Virginia) EC2**; `usquotes-api.webullfintech.com` also us-east-1.
- `trading-api.kalshi.com` → 18.220.113.153 / 3.134.254.194 = **AWS us-east-2 (Ohio) EC2**; Glassnode's monitor states Kalshi origin is us-east-2 Ohio and Polymarket's is eu-west-2 London (https://latency.glassnode.com/prediction-markets/about). QuantVPS's "Chicago 1 ms" claim measures a CloudFront edge (https://www.quantvps.com/blog/kalshi-servers-location).
- us-east-1 ↔ us-east-2 ≈ 14 ms RTT (https://www.economize.cloud/resources/aws/latency/us-east-1-vs-us-east-2/, live at https://www.cloudping.co/). Recommendation: **AWS us-east-1 (or Lightsail/EC2 in Virginia)** when executing through Webull; **us-east-2** if the direct-Kalshi executor becomes primary. Non-AWS options (Vultr/Hetzner Ashburn) add a few ms; none of Picador's strategies are latency-critical except S4.

**Clock**
- Webull requests carry `x-timestamp` in ISO8601 UTC; the server-side skew tolerance is **UNVERIFIED** (not in public docs). Use chrony (Ubuntu docs: https://ubuntu.com/server/docs/how-to/networking/chrony-client/), and on AWS point at Amazon Time Sync `169.254.169.123` with `prefer iburst minpoll 4 maxpoll 4` (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/configure-ec2-ntp.html). Export chrony's `System time` offset to Prometheus and halt trading when |offset| > 100 ms (S4 needs the expiry minute exactly).

**Secrets on a headless box**
- Put App Secret and the 2FA token directory under systemd credentials: `systemd-creds encrypt` (host key or TPM2) into `/etc/credstore.encrypted/` (0600 root) and `LoadCredentialEncrypted=` in the unit; credentials are decrypted only into the service's private tmpfs and vanish on stop (https://systemd.io/CREDENTIALS/, https://www.systemshardening.com/articles/linux/systemd-credentials-hardening/). No `.env` files, no secrets in the journal. Point `WEBULL_OPENAPI_TOKEN_DIR` at a `StateDirectory=` owned by the service user (0700).
- Unit hardening: `DynamicUser=` or dedicated user, `ProtectSystem=strict`, `ProtectHome=yes`, `PrivateTmp=yes`, `NoNewPrivileges=yes`, `RestrictAddressFamilies=AF_INET AF_INET6`, `IPAddressAllow=` for Webull/Kalshi/Deribit CIDRs, `CapabilityBoundingSet=`, `SystemCallFilter=@system-service`; aim for `systemd-analyze security` score ≤ 2. Enable unattended-upgrades, SSH keys only, fail2ban, and Webull's 2FA on the account.
- Reset App Key/Secret immediately if the box is ever compromised (help 10512); Webull has no read-only scope for individuals (**UNVERIFIED** whether scoped keys exist — plan §5 rule 12 assumes they might).

**Kill switch and risk controls (FIA best practices, July 2024, https://www.fia.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/FIA_WP_AUTOMATED%20TRADING%20RISK%20CONTROLS_FINAL_0.pdf)**
- Pre-trade: maximum order size per instrument (§1.x), price collars around fair value, **repeated automated execution limits** (§3.2: after N identical fill-and-re-enter cycles the strategy disables itself until a human re-enables), message throttles (§3.4) well below Webull's 600/60 s, intraday position limits as a "speed bump".
- Kill switch (§1.5): "immediately disables all trading activity ... preventing the ability to enter new orders and cancelling all working orders", optionally allowing risk-reducing orders. Implement two: an in-process one (RiskGovernor) and an out-of-band one (a second tiny service or a phone-triggered Telegram command that cancels every open Webull order via a separately stored key and stops the unit). Test it weekly.
- Cancel-on-disconnect (§1.4): Webull does not document COD for OpenAPI (**UNVERIFIED**). Because orders are DAY, a crashed bot leaves resting orders live until 12:10 AM (see §8). Picador must cancel all orders on shutdown, on MQTT/gRPC silence, and from a watchdog if the main process dies.
- Post-trade (§4.1): treat the gRPC order/position event stream as the "drop copy" and reconcile every fill against it and against daily executions; alert on any discrepancy.

**Monitoring stack for one person**
- Prometheus + Alertmanager with a "Watchdog" always-firing alert routed to a dead-man's switch (healthchecks.io or self-hosted Healthchecks; https://healthchecks.io/docs/, https://blog.ediri.io/how-to-set-up-a-dead-mans-switch-in-prometheus), so silence itself pages you. Uptime Kuma push monitors are the lightweight alternative (https://bex.co/blog/2026/09/11/on-call-two-person-platform-uptime-kuma-ntfy-alertmanager). Page via Telegram plus one independent channel (ntfy/SMS). Alerts that must exist: token INVALID, MQTT/gRPC silence > 30 s, clock offset, order-reject rate, unreconciled fills, daily loss lockout, open orders after category close, disk/CPU.

## 7. Failure modes documented by bot operators

- Avellaneda–Stoikov Kalshi market maker, 8 paper sessions, −$130 on ~4,400 fills: spreads clipped to the book (1¢ quotes vs multi-cent adverse selection), inverted quotes on 1¢ books, inventory skew silenced instead of skewing, forced time-based exits ("the cure was worse than the disease"), and **no fee model at all**; only passive round trips were profitable (https://github.com/AmarRSehgal/prediction-mm).
- PillarLab "failed twice": (1) polled REST every 10 s and traded stale prices; (2) valuation built only from Kalshi's own history, i.e., no independent signal; fixes were WebSocket ingestion, an external valuation engine, **category-level exposure caps** for correlated strike ladders, daily loss halt, and a remote manual kill switch (https://pillarlabai.com/blog/how-to-build-kalshi-trading-bot/).
- Multi-strategy bot with a 93% win rate still lost as taker on efficient series; a wrong dict key made a bot believe it had zero positions and keep re-entering; "Protogen Max" went 0–32 on an uncalibrated model before adopting base-rate divergence, fractional Kelly and a 20% exposure cap (search summaries; repos: https://github.com/rudebotAI/rudebot-predictions/pull/3, https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/kalshi-trading-bots-complete-guide).
- Bartlett & O'Hara, "Adverse Selection in Prediction Markets: Evidence from Kalshi" (41.6M trades, 478k markets): informed price impact is higher in single-name markets than broad-based ones; traders systematically overbet YES; broad-based (index/crypto) products price more efficiently (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6615739, https://law.stanford.edu/2026/04/21/adverse-selection-in-prediction-markets-evidence-from-kalshi/). Implication: Picador's edge must come from an external model, not from Kalshi's own tape; and the YES-overbetting bias suggests NO-side fair-value trades are structurally cheaper.
- Turbine's synthesis: bots trade ~89×/day vs 2.2 for humans; a latency bot took $271.5k in 30 days from Polymarket 5-minute contracts; accounts under ~$10k lose on average, and adverse-selection costs are only absorbed at ~$100k+ (https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/why-prediction-market-trades-get-picked-off-2026). This argues against the plan's $2–5k start being anything but a measurement budget.
- Journalists' experiment: an LLM-driven bot lost $3,000 on Polymarket/Kalshi, driven by fees and overconfident probabilities (https://cybernews.com/ai-news/viral-ai-trading-debunk-model-lost-money-polymarket-kalshi/).
- Guardrails to encode (all are configuration, not discretion): daily loss lockout persisted to disk; per-category and per-hour exposure caps; "N consecutive losses → cooldown"; no size increases without Wilson-bound evidence; no manual overrides while a lockout is active; a weekly written review; Webull's **Voluntary Opt-Out** (Account → Event Contract account → Voluntary Opt-Out) as the human-side circuit breaker (FAQ 11052).

## 8. Webull Event Contract account risk controls (what is and is not published)

Published:
- Per-order caps in the broker guidance: **$50,000 per order and 500,000 contracts**; prices $0.01–$0.99, some markets $0.0001; fractional 0.01 contracts in some markets; trading halts at `stop_trading_time` with no new orders or modifications (https://developer.webull.com/apis/docs/broker-api/event-contract-guidance/).
- Buying power: cash = settled cash BP − provisional cash; margin = lesser of overnight/intraday BP excess − provisional cash; **if the linked margin account is PDT-flagged, event BP is limited to the excess above $25,000** (https://www.webull.com/learn/courseware/fZidum/Trading-Event-Contracts, FAQ 11053). No leverage, so no event-side margin calls; but a margin call in the linked account removes event BP and, under Rule 3.3(f), a negative FCM account can be liquidated by Webull.
- "Market professionals must consult compliance officers"; Voluntary Opt-Out; rule33@kalshi.com disclosure for existing Kalshi accounts (FAQ 11052).
- Webull's in-app FAQ 11053 (https://www.webull.com/help/faq/11053-Trading-Event-Contracts) lists **Quick (market) orders, IOC, FOK, GTC, GTD, and "Day: cancels at 12:10 AM next day"**, and says crypto/economics/sports trade 24/7 — while the courseware page and the OpenAPI docs say LIMIT/DAY only and crypto weekdays 8–18 ET. Two operational consequences for the plan: (a) **DAY orders live until 00:10 ET, not until the 16:00/18:00 category close** — the "re-arm at 8:00 ET" rule must also cancel at close; (b) the crypto hours and GTC/IOC availability through the API should be re-tested in sandbox/paper rather than assumed; the broker guidance says crypto is "not subject to typical trading hour restrictions".

Not published (**UNVERIFIED**): per-market or per-account concentration caps, automatic liquidation of event positions, position-limit rejection codes, cancel-on-disconnect, read-only API scopes, and Webull's handling of Kalshi "under review" markets (Kalshi's Rule 7.1 caps review at 24 h; Webull shows no separate policy).
